# 1. BI Fundamentals

## What is Business Intelligence?
Business Intelligence is the set of processes, technologies and practices used to transform data into understandable information for analysis and decision-making.

Typical BI outputs:
- Reports
- Dashboards
- Scorecards
- KPIs
- Alerts
- Ad-hoc analysis

## Why BI Exists
Businesses collect large amounts of operational data, but raw records do not directly answer questions such as:
- How many vehicles were sold this month?
- Which dealer has the highest service revenue?
- What is the warranty claim rate?
- Which vehicle model has the highest service frequency?

BI creates a structured path from raw data to business insight.

## BI Lifecycle
```text
Collect → Store → Transform → Model → Govern → Visualize → Analyze → Act
```

## BI Layers
1. Source systems
2. Ingestion
3. Storage
4. Transformation
5. Warehouse/lakehouse
6. Semantic layer
7. BI visualization
8. Consumers

## BI vs Analytics
BI often focuses on recurring reporting and decision support. Analytics is broader and can include exploratory, predictive and prescriptive analysis.

## Important Terms
**Dimension:** descriptive context such as vehicle model or dealer.

**Measure:** numeric value that can be aggregated, such as revenue.

**KPI:** a business metric tied to a defined objective.

**Dashboard:** visual interface containing related analytical information.

## Automobile Example 🚗
A manufacturer combines vehicle sales, dealer service orders and connected-car telemetry. Executives view monthly sales, service revenue, warranty rate and vehicle utilization from a BI dashboard.

## Best Practices
- Define business questions first.
- Use governed metric definitions.
- Keep source data separate from presentation models.
- Document KPI formulas.
- Make dashboards actionable.
